## 3. The Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) Analysis

We decompose the state vector $\mathbf{x}^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ into two orthogonal components:
$$\mathbf{x}^{(t)} = \mathbf{s}^{(t)} + \mathbf{\eta}^{(t)}$$
where:
* $\mathbf{s}^{(t)} = \mathbf{x}^{(t)}_{\text{SAT}} - \mathbf{x}^{(t)}_{\text{UNSAT}}$ is the **Target Signal** (localized initially at $v_0$).
* $\mathbf{\eta}^{(t)} = \mathbf{x}^{(t)} - \frac{1}{n} \sum_v x_v^{(t)} \mathbf{1}$ represents the **Local Symmetric Noise** generated by the $n-1$ non-defect vertices.

```

• Initial energy: ||s^(0)||^2 ≤ O(1/n^2).              • Background energy: ||η^(0)||^2 = Θ(1).
```

### The Amplification Dynamics:
Suppose an operator $\Psi$ attempts to amplify the perturbation by a gain factor $\gamma > 1$ per step:

   $$\|\mathbf{\eta}^{(t+1)}\| \ge \gamma \|\mathbf{\eta}^{(t)}\|$$
